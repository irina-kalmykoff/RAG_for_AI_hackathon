RAG exercise: run sheet and answer key

A 10–15 minute hands-on activity. Participants ask a chatbot about a fictional company twice: once on its own, once with excerpts from the annual report. They need the exercise page and any chatbot (ChatGPT works without an account). No installs, no code.

Run sheet

TimeWhat happens
0–2 minFrame it. “Marlow & Finch is invented. No AI has ever seen its figures. Let's see what happens when we ask anyway.”
2–5 minQuestion A, step 2: everyone asks directly. Collect a few answers out loud. Compare them: different people usually get different numbers, or a polite refusal.
5–9 minQuestion A, steps 3–4: search, paste, ask in a new chat. Everyone should now get the same, correct answer with excerpt citations.
9–13 minQuestion B (the trap). Let them run it with the default search, then reveal the missing excerpt.
13–15 minDebrief (three points below). Fast finishers can try the Bonus question.

Answer key

Question A · the clear win

What was the operating profit in 2025, and why did it change compared with 2024?

Answer: Operating profit (EBIT) fell from €5.2m to €3.9m, down 25%; operating margin went from 12.3% to 8.0%. Main reason: green arabica beans cost 38% more and only 40% was hedged, so cost of sales rose from 60% to 64% of revenue. The 9% price increase only started in April, so it covered part of the rise. Higher staff costs and rent for three new cafés added to operating expenses.
Excerpts needed
E4 or E3 for the figures, E6 for the reason; E7, E8 add detail.
Default search finds
E4, E3, E6: enough for a good answer.
Direct prompt
Either invents numbers (often confidently, sometimes with a plausible story about coffee prices, which is a lucky guess) or says it has no data. Both make the point.
Question B · the trap

Is the company at risk with its bank loan?

Answer: Yes. At year-end leverage was 2.9x against a 3.0x covenant limit, so just inside. After year-end the company bought Café Aurora for €6.5m on the credit facility, which takes pro forma leverage to 3.6x. The bank waived the June 2026 test, but if the ratio is still above 3.0x on 30 September 2026, the lenders can demand early repayment.
Excerpts needed
E10 (covenant) and E13 (events after year-end).
Default search finds
Only E12 (risk management: “liquidity risk is low”) and E10 (2.9x, within the limit). E13 never uses the words “bank”, “loan” or “risk”, so a keyword search misses it.
What they'll get
A confident, well-cited and wrong “no, the company is fine” answer.
The reveal: ask them to search for covenant instead, or open “Browse the full report” and tick E13. Run it again. “The AI didn't lie. It answered from the pages we gave it. The mistake happened at the lookup step.”
Bonus · the calculation check

What is the interest coverage ratio for 2025 (operating profit divided by interest expense)?

Answer: €3.9m ÷ €1.3m = 3.0x (2024: 5.2 ÷ 0.95 ≈ 5.5x).
Excerpts needed
E4 or E3 (EBIT) and E11 or E10 (interest expense).
Watch for
Using net finance costs (€1.05m) from the income statement gives 3.7x, which flatters the ratio. Ask: “Which number did it use, and would you have used the same one in Excel?” The citations make this easy to check.

Debrief: three points

  1. Without documents, the AI guesses. It sounds equally sure whether it knows or not. For company-specific figures, never trust a direct answer.
  2. With the right documents, it becomes a fast, careful reader. It quotes figures, shows its sources, and different people get the same answer.
  3. The answer is only as good as what was retrieved. Question B was wrong because the key page was never found. Always ask: what did it look at, and what might be missing?

Excel analogy: RAG is filtering to the right rows before you write the formula. A perfect formula on the wrong rows still gives the wrong total.

Practical notes

  • If ChatGPT searches the web in step 2, that's fine: it finds nothing, because the company doesn't exist.
  • Tell people to open a new chat for step 4, so the earlier guess doesn't leak into the answer.
  • Chatbots vary. A cautious model may refuse in step 2 instead of inventing numbers. Point out that a refusal is honest, but still leaves you without an answer.
  • In a real RAG tool (Copilot on SharePoint, ChatGPT with uploaded files), steps 3 and 4 happen automatically and invisibly. That makes the Question B lesson more important: you don't see what was retrieved unless it cites its sources.

Marlow & Finch Coffee Roasters (fictional) keeps its 2025 sales in one file: 540 orders. The company's new “ask your documents” assistant lets anyone type a question about the file.

Behind the scenes, the assistant does what many such tools do: it cuts the file into pieces of fixed size, searches for the pieces that best match your question, and passes only those three pieces to the chatbot.

      Why did it go wrong?
      • Only 3 of 23 pieces: a total needs every row. The search found about 80 of 540 rows.
      • No header: only the first piece has the column names, so the other pieces are bare numbers.
      • Rows cut in half: fixed-size pieces ignore row boundaries, so rows at the edges lose their amount or their customer.
      • Words vs. data: the file says 2025-12-…, never “December”, so a keyword search for December finds nothing useful.
      • No warning: the chatbot can't see what was left out, so a partial sum can look like the answer.

      Asking a document assistant to total a big spreadsheet is like asking a colleague who read three random pages of your ledger.

      All data is fictional. Test answers came from an AI model given the same three pieces; your chatbot may answer differently, which is part of the lesson.
